fn gain_controller() -> GainController2
Expand description

AGC2 as this endpoint configures it — the loudness contract lives here.

Target: RFC 7874’s −19 dBm0 active-speech level, i.e. −22 dBFS RMS ± 6 dB measured above 300 Hz. Adaptive digital gain ALONE cannot reach it, which is why this is not Default::default():

  • The adaptive stage’s settled target is -(headroom_db + saturation-protector headroom) = −17…−30 dBFS by design — a peak-safe trim, not a loudness normaliser. In Chrome/Discord it rides on top of an input-volume controller that drives the OS mic gain (P5, now wired — see Apm::set_applied_input_volume / Apm::recommended_input_volume and call::audio_tx for the platform round trip), but that controller’s recommendation is orthogonal to this digital math (sonora’s GainController2::process never reads the applied/ recommended input volume — verified against 0.2.0 source), so the fixed + adaptive digital stages below still have to do the whole job whenever no OS provider is resolved (Linux today) or a write is gated off (AGC disabled, muted, unavailable device). fixed_digital is the standard make-up stage for exactly this, and it feeds AGC2’s own limiter (−1 dBFS, 5:1), so raising it trades headroom, not clipping.
  • max_output_noise_level_dbfs clamps the adaptive gain to ceiling − post-NS noise floor. At the −50 dBFS default an ordinary mic is allowed 5–10 dB regardless of max_gain_db; −42 unclamps it. Going further (−40 and up) starts pumping room noise between words.

The two knobs are not additive, and that is why the make-up gain is 7 dB and not 12. Measured separately, each is worth ~+10 dB; together they are worth ~+19, because unclamping the noise ceiling is what lets the adaptive stage do the work the fixed stage was standing in for. 12 dB on top of the unclamped ceiling put this repo’s speech-shaped excitation at −13.3 dBFS RMS — past the band’s loud edge and hotter than Discord-class. 7 dB targets the clean- excitation case at ~−18 dBFS, the top of the band: that case has the best SNR any input will have, so the noise clamp binds least and it is the LOUDEST the chain gets. Real, noisier mics let the clamp bind and land further down the band — which is the right way round.

One function so the live config and Apm::set_agc_enabled’s rebuild cannot drift apart — a toggle that reinstated Default::default() would silently undo all of the above.
