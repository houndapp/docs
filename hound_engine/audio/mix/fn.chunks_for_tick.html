fn chunks_for_tick(depth_ms: Option<u32>, late_us: u128) -> usize
Expand description

How many mixed chunks this tick should push, from the sink’s reported ring depth (depth_ms, sampled BEFORE the push) and how late the tick fired (late_us).

This is the closed loop around OUTPUT_CUSHION_MS. The bus produces on a software 10 ms clock while the DAC drains on its own crystal, so one chunk per tick is only right if the two rates match exactly. They never do: tens of ppm is normal (far worse on an OS-resampled BT/HFP route), and open-loop the ring walks to one of the two ends within minutes — dry (continuous fine-grained dropouts, which diagnose() then misattributes to the device/route) or full (output latency pinned at the ring depth, with the overproduction silently discarded).

  • depth ABOVE the dead band ⇒ push nothing. The chunk is not mixed at all, so no audio is lost: the slots simply keep it and the whole playout timeline slips 10 ms later, which is exactly the correction a slow DAC needs.
  • depth BELOW it ⇒ push the deficit as extra chunks (bounded by MAX_CHUNKS_PER_TICK). The extra chunks come from the slots’ carry, which is where a stall’s un-drained backlog sits — so catching the ring up also drains the backlog instead of leaving it as permanent added latency.
  • a LATE tick means the device drained for late_us with no refill; replay those chunks even when the depth reading has not caught up yet, so a scheduling stall costs a one-time gap rather than the cushion for the rest of the call.

depth_ms == None (a sink that cannot report depth — the synthetic/null sinks) keeps the previous strictly-open-loop behaviour: exactly one chunk per tick.
