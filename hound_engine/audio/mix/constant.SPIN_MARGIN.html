How long before a tick’s deadline TickPacer stops sleeping and busy-waits.

The trade is spin cost against release precision. Every coarse wait (CoarseWait) guarantees only a lower bound on the delay; the overshoot is the OS timer granularity, so a pure sleep(deadline - now) releases the tick a granularity-sized quantum LATE, every tick — 15.6 ms on default-configured Windows (worse than the 10 ms period itself), and hundreds of µs on a loaded mac/Linux box. Ending each wait in a busy loop makes the release precise to within the loop’s own iteration cost regardless of the wait clock.

1.5 ms on every platform. On Windows it is sized off the worst overshoot the bus can still see from CoarseWait: ~0.5 ms from the high-resolution waitable timer, or a 1 ms timeBeginPeriod quantum on the pre-1803 fallback — a full quantum with headroom either way, so the busy loop is entered before the deadline rather than after it. A smaller non-Windows margin was tried and measured out: at 300 µs a loaded mac released 2.3 ms late on average — macOS timer coalescing gives short sleeps multi-ms slop — so the margin that keeps the release precise is the same one everywhere. It is also the worst-case busy time per tick (15% of one core, only while a call runs the bus); the actual figure is lower, since the wait only spins the part of the margin the preceding coarse wait did not consume, and the SPIN_YIELD_FLOOR band yields rather than spins.
