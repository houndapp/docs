§Agent-readable video-quality debug report

The video twin of crate::audio_report: a pure function — video_report — that turns one StatsSnapshot into a compact, deterministic, paste-able text block that pre-attributes a degraded tile to a pipeline stage. A reader (human or agent) sees a cause, not a wall of counters: a diagnosis: headline names the most likely fault with the numbers that justify it, and a per-stage table flags every signal ok / HIGH / BAD.

§The pipeline stages (any can freeze a tile)

SEND     source ─▶ encoder ─▶ publish channel ─▶ uplink ─▶ relay
RECEIVE  relay ─▶ downlink ─▶ keyframe gate ─▶ decoder ─▶ playout ─▶ render

One client observes BOTH halves: its own send path (invisible to every receive-side gauge — a dead camera or a jammed uplink reads perfectly healthy locally, which is exactly why the send rules come first) and, per peer and per stream (cam vs screen), everything from the downlink rightward.

§Why the split matters

Video fails in ways audio does not. A single lost frame breaks the reference chain until the next keyframe, so “arriving but not decoding” is a distinct state from “not arriving”; the encoder is a rung-driven consumer, so “encoded ≪ captured” is only a fault relative to min(capture rate, rung fps); and a screen share deliberately captures NOTHING while its content is static. The rules below encode those asymmetries so the report never cries wolf at a healthy static share, a BWE pause, or a track the peer simply stopped publishing.

§Windowed, never cumulative

Every rule reads per-window signals (*_fps, *_window) so the verdict describes the LAST second. The cumulative totals in the snapshot are for the panel; a rules engine fed cumulative counters latches on the first bad frame of the call and never recovers.

Pure + deterministic ⇒ unit-tested per fault class, which is what keeps the agent-facing format honest.

Constants§

AUDIO_ONLY_RUNG 🔒
The rung label the adaptation tick writes when congestion has paused video entirely. Every capture/encode rule is inert on a paused track — the encoder is supposed to be silent — and the pause itself is the Diagnosis.
CAPTURE_KEEPUP_RATIO 🔒
Capture rate below this fraction of the rung’s negotiated fps is a struggling source (thermal throttle, a contended camera, a compositor starving the capturer). Half the negotiated rate is the point at which motion visibly judders rather than merely looking soft.
DECODER_CHURN_WINDOW 🔒
Decoder (re)builds in one window that count as churn. One is a normal (re)start; two means the sender’s codec byte is flapping or decode keeps failing, and each rebuild throws away the reference chain.
DECODE_KEEPUP_RATIO 🔒
Decode rate below this fraction of the ARRIVAL rate: frames reach the decoder and do not come out the other side.
DECODE_P95_HIGH_MS 🔒
p95 of one VideoDecoder::decode call (ms) above which decode is the bottleneck. Same budget argument as ENCODE_P95_HIGH_MS — and a software fallback (AV1 with no hardware block) blowing past it also starves the audio fill path.
DWELL_HIGH_MS 🔒
p95 playout dwell (ms) above which frames are queueing instead of being paced. The receive playout stage is a SHALLOW presentation pacer (~40 ms target), so a p95 several times that is a backlog building toward a collapse-drop.
ENCODE_KEEPUP_RATIO 🔒
Encoder output below this fraction of what it was FED (min(capture, rung fps)) means access units are being dropped rather than paced — the encoder is failing or rejecting frames, not merely rate-limiting.
ENCODE_P95_HIGH_MS 🔒
p95 of one HW-encode call (ms) above which the encoder is the bottleneck: over half a 30 fps frame budget (33 ms) spent inside a single encode leaves no headroom for capture + publish, and on a shared CPU it competes with the RT audio path.
KEYFRAME_NAG_STORM 🔒
Recovery keyframe requests this receiver SENT in one window that count as a nag storm. The pipeline throttles its own requests to one per 2 s, so reaching two in a single window means the stream is breaking as fast as it is being repaired.
MEANINGFUL_FPS 🔒
Minimum expected encode rate before the keep-up ratio is judged at all. Below a handful of frames per second the denominator is too small to distinguish a drop from ordinary pacing jitter, and a 1 fps static share would trip on rounding.
PLAYOUT_DROP_WINDOW 🔒
Decoded frames the playout buffer may discard per window before it counts as a fault. The buffer legitimately supersedes a stale frame around a pause/resume or a layer switch; three in one second is the render stage throwing away work the decoder already paid for.
PLI_STORM_WINDOW 🔒
Keyframe requests from remote receivers, per window, that count as a PLI storm. The producer coalesces duplicates through its own throttle, so two DISTINCT requests in one second means multiple receivers independently lost their reference.
PUBLISH_QUEUE_HIGH_MS 🔒
p95 dwell (ms) on the bounded publish channel above which the uplink — not the encoder — is the constraint. Three 30 fps frame times: past that the channel is running at its depth and the next burst drops.
RECV_LOSS_HIGH_PCT 🔒
Receive-side loss (percent of the frames that should have arrived) above which the downlink is the fault. Lower than the audio threshold on purpose: a concealed Opus frame is a blip, a lost video frame corrupts every dependent frame after it.
REMOTE_LOSS_HIGH_PCT 🔒
Worst receiver-reported downstream loss (percent) that counts as a lossy uplink. Video has no PLC: at this rate the reference chain breaks faster than keyframes can repair it, which is also roughly where the congestion controller starts backing off.
STALL_FPS 🔒
Frames/sec below which a stream counts as delivering NOTHING (send or receive). Deliberately under 1 fps: a static screen share legitimately re-emits its cached frame at the 1 s static-refresh cadence, and a report that called that “stalled” would be wrong on every idle share.

decoder_unbuildable 🔒
No decoder for the sender’s wire codec could be CONSTRUCTED here. Judged from the build failure itself rather than from throughput, because on the rate counters alone it is indistinguishable from a wedged downlink, the keyframe gate and a decoder falling behind all at once — and none of their remedies apply. A latched gauge, not a window delta: the decode loop only re-attempts every few seconds, so a per-window count reads 0 in most windows the fault is present.

diagnose_video_detail

Rules are ordered by severity, and within that by causality — a rule that MANUFACTURES another’s signature is checked first, so the report names the cause instead of its consequence (a wedged downlink is not “decode falling behind”; a paused rung is not “the encoder died”).
