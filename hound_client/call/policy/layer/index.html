Receive-side layer policy: per-tile demand + the roster seed, the spatial layer-switch hysteresis, the decode-session budget, pipeline restart pacing, recovery-PLI throttling, and per-stream playback volumes.

Structs§

DecodeBudget 🔒
The live cap on how many remote video tiles may hold a decoder, walked between MIN_DECODE_SESSIONS and the platform’s static ceiling (hound_engine::api::Engine::decode_session_budget) against whether the RT audio mix thread is keeping its schedule. Fed to hound_media::allocate_video’s decode_budget, which does the actual cutting — from the BOTTOM of its priority order, so the tiles that lose their decoder are the ones nobody is watching.

Per-(peer, source) hysteresis for the spatial-layer subscription decision. Pure (no IO) so the policy is unit-tested in isolation. Self::observe takes the raw per-tile demand and returns the committed demand the subscribe loop should act on. The gating is directional along the LayerAxis ladder:
PliThrottle 🔒
Rate-limits a pipeline’s recovery keyframe requests (RECOVERY_PLI_MIN_INTERVAL). Pure; unit-tested.
RestartPacer 🔒
Pure restart-pacing policy for reaped pipelines (unit-tested). A HEALTHY death — the pipeline delivered frames and lived ≥ RESTART_PACE — restarts immediately and resets the track’s ladder (a live stream hit a transient fault; recover fast). An UNHEALTHY death (instant kill, or a subscription that never delivered anything) climbs an exponential ladder, so a track the relay/producer keeps wedging retries forever at a decaying, bounded rate instead of churning the shared connection. Self::ready_at is what the reconcile consults.
StreamVolumes 🔒
Per-remote-audio-stream playback gain, keyed (peer, source) — the tile right-click volume/mute control. Each audio pipeline holds its stream’s gain CELL (an AtomicU32 of f32 bits, loaded lock-free per frame); the map only locks on the ~UI-rate Self::set / pipeline-start Self::cell paths. Lives on [ConnectionParams] so a volume survives reconnects and resubscribes (the cell is looked up by key, not by pipeline instance).
TileDemand 🔒
This receiver’s tile demand: what each RENDERED tile reported, plus whether this receiver reports tile demand at all. Pure (a plain map + a latch), so the roster-seeding rule below is unit-tested rather than inferred from the app.

Constants§

DECODE_LIMITATION_SUSTAIN 🔒
How long the receiver must look decode-LIMITED before a single slot is cut.
DECODE_RECOVERY_INTERVAL 🔒
How long the receiver must look CLEAN before one slot is handed back. 60 s — Jitsi’s figure for the same decision, and it is the whole anti-oscillation argument of this controller: see DecodeBudget.
DECODE_TICK_LATE_LIMIT_US 🔒
Mix-tick lateness (p95, µs) at or above which this receiver is treated as decode LIMITED. The same threshold the audio debug report’s rules engine calls a “tick slip”, derived from it so the budget and the diagnosis can never disagree about whether a box is struggling.
DECODE_WARMUP 🔒
How long after a receiver is constructed no cut may land — 10 s, and it is not politeness, it is that the lateness of the first few seconds is CAUSED BY THE JOIN and clears on its own.
LAYER_SWITCH_DEBOUNCE 🔒
How long a tile’s demand for the LOW layer must HOLD before we downgrade its video subscription. A switch tears down + reopens a moq subscription on the connection shared with audio; doing it on every transient tile resize (the Flutter view relays out after a (re)join, firing a burst of sizes that flap source→low→source within a second) churns the connection’s stream state and disrupts AUDIO delivery (an “audio is sacred” violation). Holding the demand until it is durable collapses that burst to at most one switch. A downgrade only ever saves bandwidth nobody asked for, so it can afford to be slow. Upgrades are governed by LAYER_UPGRADE_DEBOUNCE instead.
LAYER_SWITCH_DWELL 🔒
Minimum dwell on a layer after an actuated switch before the next DOWNGRADE may commit. The debounce alone only filters flaps shorter than itself; a demand that alternates slower than the debounce (a stage↔filmstrip focus toggle re-laying tiles out every couple of seconds) would pass it and thrash the subscription anyway. The dwell bounds actuated churn to one downgrade per window per stream regardless of the flap period; a held demand still actuates the moment the dwell expires. Upgrades skip it (see LAYER_UPGRADE_DEBOUNCE) — that stays storm-safe because an upgrade can only ever follow a committed (dwell-gated) downgrade, so the dwell still bounds the alternation rate to one down/up pair per window.
LAYER_UPGRADE_DEBOUNCE 🔒
How long a tile’s demand for the SOURCE layer must hold before we UPGRADE. Asymmetric with LAYER_SWITCH_DEBOUNCE on purpose: an upgrade is the direct answer to a user expanding a tile and they are staring at the result, so this only has to absorb a relayout flap — a tile briefly large for a frame or two while the grid reshuffles, i.e. tens of milliseconds. ~200 ms is several frame periods of margin yet reads as instant.
MIN_DECODE_SESSIONS 🔒
Floor on the live decode budget — Jitsi’s MIN_LAST_N. Below three remote videos a gallery has stopped being a video call, and the audio this protects is long since safe.
RECOVERY_PLI_MIN_INTERVAL 🔒
Minimum spacing between the recovery keyframe requests a video pipeline sends outside the subscribe-time nag — on a mid-stream stall or after a backpressure drop broke decode continuity. The producer coalesces duplicates through its own throttle; this just keeps a long producer pause from turning into a control-plane drip.
RESTART_PACE 🔒
Base restart pace for a reaped pipeline, and the first rung of the escalation ladder: an UNHEALTHY death (died fast, or never delivered a frame) waits this long, doubling per consecutive unhealthy death up to RESTART_PACE_MAX.
RESTART_PACE_MAX 🔒
Ladder ceiling: a persistently-dead track (producer gone dark, relay routing wedged) keeps retrying forever — resubscribes are how it eventually heals — but never faster than this once the ladder tops out.

Functions§

layer_axis 🔒
The subscription an allocation selects. Hidden is LayerAxis::Off: the demand still travels to the PRODUCER (its cap sizes the shared low layer when every requester is hidden — hound_media::plan_layers), but this receiver subscribes to nothing for it.
unbuilt_tile_demand 🔒
What a roster peer whose tile the UI never built demands: the same thing a tile that mounted and disposed reports — Hidden at the cheapest rung for that content (DemandBucketer::hidden sends its last size, or zero if it never had one, and the ladder ignores the size of a hidden tile). Keeping the two identical is what makes “never built” and “scrolled away” one case rather than two.
video_source 🔒
Map the control-plane Source onto hound-media’s video content kind — the axis the demand ladder prices against. Camera and screen compress differently and degrade differently (a small camera tile drops resolution, a small screen tile drops frame rate), so every rung decision needs it. Microphone has no video ladder; it is treated as camera content, which no caller can reach — the tile-demand and low-layer paths are both video-only.
