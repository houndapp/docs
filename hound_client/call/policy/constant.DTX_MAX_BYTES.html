The DTX/coded size threshold. Owned by the codec that produces the sizes (hound_engine::api::DTX_MAX_BYTES) and measured against the live encoder there, so the publisher, the receiver and the codec cannot drift apart. Largest encoded payload (bytes) this encoder produces that is DTX output rather than coded signal — the one number that separates “the encoder gave up on this frame” from “the encoder is spending bits”, without anyone having to look at the audio.

It lives here, on the codec, because only the encoder’s measured behaviour can justify it — and it is deliberately ONE constant for the whole stack: the publisher’s silence gate uses it to decide what not to send, the receiver to decide that an arriving frame carries no speech (so the peer’s level meter must read 0) and that the hold is worth draining. One constant, one meaning.

Measured on this encoder (24 kbps, OpusProfile::Voice), the size distribution is sharply bimodal with nothing in between:

inputsteady-state packet sizes
bit-exact digital silence1 B, with an 8 B SILK refresh every ~20 frames
room tone, −70…−55 dBFS1 B, with a fully coded 36–57 B refresh every ~21 frames
coded speech / any real signal≥36 B

So 10 clears the 8 B digital-silence refresh with margin and sits far below the smallest coded frame ever observed. the_dtx_threshold_straddles_the_encoders_own_gap re-measures both edges against the live encoder rather than against literals.

Note what the table says about the refresh: it is only ~8 B when the input is bit-exact zeros. Give the encoder any noise floor at all — i.e. a real microphone — and the periodic refresh is a fully coded frame that lands well above this threshold. That is why the publisher’s gate needs hysteresis on the way down (SilenceGate::RESUME_AFTER) rather than treating one coded frame as speech.
