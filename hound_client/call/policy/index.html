hound_client::call

Module policy 

Source
Expand description

Pure, unit-tested call policy — the sans-IO decision layer. Inputs (sizes, instants, samples, roster caps) in, decisions out. Nothing here touches sockets, clocks, or devices, so every policy is pinned by unit tests without a live source. Split by domain:

  • codec — negotiation + the mid-call CodecSwitch and wire mappings.
  • capture — source liveness / first-frame watchdogs, resize debounce, device open-retry, encoder-rebuild throttle, low-rung geometry.
  • audio — mic route warmup / dead-silence detection, publisher-side silence suppression, receiver gap/PLC math + level reporting.
  • layer — tile demand + roster seeding, layer-switch hysteresis, the decode-session budget, restart pacing, playback volumes.

audio 🔒
Audio policy, both directions: capture-side mic route warmup + dead-silence detection, per-stream playback gain application, publisher-side silence suppression (SilenceGate), and the receiver’s gap classification / PLC math / level reporting / cadence diagnostics.
capture 🔒
Capture-source policy: aspect-preserving low-rung screen geometry, the source-liveness probe throttle and first-frame watchdog, the dry-restart give-up bound, resize debounce, capture-device open retry + log dedupe, and the mid-stream encoder-rebuild throttle.
codec 🔒
Codec choice: negotiation against the roster’s common decode caps, the control-plane/wire mappings, and the mid-call CodecSwitch policy (downgrades land immediately, upgrades are debounced).
layer 🔒
Receive-side layer policy: per-tile demand + the roster seed, the spatial layer-switch hysteresis, the decode-session budget, pipeline restart pacing, recovery-PLI throttling, and per-stream playback volumes.

Constants§

DTX_MAX_BYTES 🔒
The DTX/coded size threshold. Owned by the codec that produces the sizes (hound_engine::api::DTX_MAX_BYTES) and measured against the live encoder there, so the publisher, the receiver and the codec cannot drift apart. Largest encoded payload (bytes) this encoder produces that is DTX output rather than coded signal — the one number that separates “the encoder gave up on this frame” from “the encoder is spending bits”, without anyone having to look at the audio.
