Classify a timestamp gap. Pure, and the ONE place the PLC-vs-silence line is drawn.

A suppressed publisher leaves gaps of a keepalive interval (~500 ms = 25 frames) and, on the resume edge, of however long it stayed quiet. Both land far past MAX_PLC_FRAMES and so classify as AudioGap::Silence: no PLC is fabricated and nothing is counted as loss, which is what keeps a quiet peer from reading as a lossy one in the stats and the audio report.

prev_was_dtx — the frame BEFORE the gap was DTX-sized — settles the case the gap size alone gets wrong: the resume edge. A publisher that resumes mid-keepalive leaves a gap of however long it had been since its last keepalive, which is uniform in (0, KEEPALIVE_INTERVAL] and so lands inside the PLC band ~20% of the time. The sender was not transmitting across it — that is what a DTX-sized predecessor means — so there is nothing to conceal: PLC would extrapolate comfort noise into the front of the first word back, and the frames would be counted as loss, putting a peer who was merely quiet into the audio report’s network-loss rule. Silence is the truth.
