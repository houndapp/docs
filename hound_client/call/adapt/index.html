Module adapt 

Source
Expand description

Sender-side BWE/congestion adaptation. The camera + screen video the client PUBLISHES adapts its bitrate/resolution to network conditions and serves keyframes on demand. The brain — [hound_media::VideoAdaptation] — is shared; this is the local IO glue:

  • MediaFeedback — the in-process form of ServerMessage::MediaFeedbackReceived fanned out of the control reader to the per-track feedback listeners.
  • AdaptShared — the atomics seam between the async 1 s adaptation TICK (owns the VideoAdaptation core, reads conn.rtt()) and the !Send capture/encode thread (owns the encoder, applies the decision per frame). One writer per field.
  • apply_adaptation_tick — the pure glue: drain backpressure + a pending PLI + the worst receiver-reported loss, advance the core, write the decision back.
  • drain_keyframe_request — the PLI→IDR transition, run BOTH the instant a RequestKeyframe arrives (the fast path) and on the tick (the safety net), so a subscribing receiver never waits a tick for its opening keyframe.
  • SendCoordinator — the shared-uplink referee. Camera and screen publish on ONE QUIC connection but adapt independently; when both are live they draw their ceilings from a single [hound_media::SendBudget] instead of summing past the wire. Inert with one publisher.
  • spawn_keyframe_listener / spawn_feedback_listener — the producer side of the PLI relay + the receiver→producer BWE loop, each FILTERED to OUR track of a given Source (Camera vs Screen) via the SessionView.

With good RTT + no loss the ladder sits at the top rung, so a healthy network is byte-identical to the pre-adaptation publish path.

Owns a [JoinHandle] and aborts the task on drop. Ties an adaptation listener’s lifetime to its publish task: when the publish task returns (or is aborted on leave/reconnect teardown), the listener is aborted too.
AdaptShared 🔒
The shared seam between the async adaptation tick (owns the sans-IO [VideoAdaptation] core + observes RTT) and the !Send capture/encode thread (owns the encoder, applies the decision per frame). Plain atomics — no lock crosses the thread/async boundary and each field has exactly one writer.
CoordState 🔒
The coordinator’s pure state: the AIMD budget plus each slot’s registration, latest desire and current cap. Every method is a (state, input) -> (state, output) transition with now_us passed in — the clock read lives one layer up in SendCoordinator, so the split policy is unit-testable.
EncodeFailStreak 🔒
Rate-limits the two “this frame produced nothing” WARN sites in encode_and_publish_adaptive (geometry-mismatch skip, encoder error). A lone failure still logs immediately at WARN; a PERSISTENT failure (encoder wedged, the geometry rebuild permanently behind) would otherwise repeat that same WARN once per captured frame — up to encode fps — without saying anything new. Once a streak is underway, only every Self::REMINDER_EVERYth failure re-alerts at WARN; the rest log at DEBUG. Reset by the next successful encode.
MediaFeedback 🔒
One receiver reception report relayed to us (the producer) for one of our published tracks — the in-process form of [ServerMessage::MediaFeedbackReceived]. The per-track feedback listener folds the loss fraction into its sender-side BWE (serve-the-worst-leg across reporters). Counts are deltas since the reporter’s previous report.
QualityReport 🔒
A receiver’s standing quality demand for one of OUR published video tracks — the in-process form of [ServerMessage::QualityRequested]. The per-track quality listener feeds it into a [hound_media::SimulcastPlanner] (per-receiver cap map); the resulting LayerPlan decides whether to spin up the shared low layer. A Capped cap below source ⇒ that receiver wants the low track.
SendCoordinator 🔒
The shared-connection send coordinator. One per process (see send_coordinator) — a call has exactly one connection, and the RAII SendSlots make it inert between calls.
SendSlot 🔒
A publisher’s registration in the SendCoordinator, released on drop (so a publish loop returning — leave, reconnect teardown, a share ending — hands the wire back to whoever is left within one tick).

Constants§

AUDIO_RESERVE_BPS 🔒
Carved off the connection budget before the video split: audio is sacred, so it takes its reserve first and the video tracks divide only what is left. Opus at the call’s profile plus packet/QUIC overhead fits well inside this.
SEND_BUDGET_INTERVAL_US 🔒
Minimum spacing between coordinator advances. The two publish loops tick at ADAPT_TICK_INTERVAL on their OWN CaptureClocks, so whichever crosses this gate first drives the advance and the other reads the fresh allocation. Slightly under 1 s so ordinary tick jitter can’t push the effective cadence out to 2 s (SendBudget’s own decrease cooldown still paces the back-off at ~1 Hz).
SEND_BUDGET_MAX_BPS 🔒
Where the coordinator’s AIMD starts, and its ceiling. Deliberately optimistic — it only ever clamps DOWN from here toward the wire, so a fast uplink never pays for the coordinator existing (the desires stay under the budget and the split hands out the full ceiling); a slow one converges within a few seconds of sustained publish drops.
SEND_BUDGET_MIN_BPS 🔒
The coordinator’s floor: below this there is no useful video left to divide (both ladders have already collapsed toward AudioOnly on their own).

Functions§

apply_adaptation_tick 🔒
The pure glue between AdaptShared (the atomics the capture thread reads/writes) and the sans-IO [VideoAdaptation] core, with RTT passed in. Drains the backpressure counters + a pending PLI + the worst receiver-reported loss, advances the core, and writes the decision back (target bitrate/fps/geometry, paused, forced keyframe). No IO here — unit-testable by driving the atomics directly (the optional SendSlot is the one seam that reads the coordinator’s clock, and it is None in those tests).
drain_keyframe_request 🔒
Turn any outstanding keyframe request into a throttle-approved forced IDR, and arm the encode loop’s force_keyframe seam when one is due. Pure (time passed in) — the throttle itself lives in [hound_media::KeyframeThrottle].
drive_video_publish 🔒
Drive one video publish loop: the 1 s adaptation tick + frame forwarding, with the live gate deciding when forwarding starts. The tick runs from the first iteration — before any subscriber — so PLIs are serviced and the BWE keeps state even while dark; only forwarding waits for proof of a receiver.
encode_and_publish_adaptive 🔒
The shared per-frame encode→adapt→publish core for both video capture loops. Retargets the encoder to the BWE’s latest bitrate (only on a real move), honours a throttle-approved keyframe (swap-clears so it fires once), downsamples to the rung’s fps, scales the frame to encoder_dims when it doesn’t already match (the caller keeps encoder_dims in sync with the live encoder — see the geometry-rebuild note below), encodes, and forwards each access unit via forward. forward returns false on backpressure (its bounded channel was full); the caller’s drop bumps dropped, which the adaptation tick reads as the congestion signal. A forced keyframe is re-armed when it is dropped, so the next captured frame re-emits it.
low_rung_frame 🔒
Downscale a capture frame to the simulcast low rung’s geometry, in whatever form low_enc consumes most cheaply.
open_encoder_laddered 🔒
Open an encoder at target, falling back DOWN the ladder ([hound_engine::types::ENCODE_PRIORITY]: AV1 → H.265 → H.264) to the first codec that opens. Safe to descend without re-checking decode caps: the negotiated target means every peer decodes it, and real decode hardware is monotonic (AV1-capable ⇒ H.265-capable ⇒ H.264), so every lower rung is decodable too. H.264 is the floor, so this normally returns None only if even H.264 won’t open — which is no longer unreachable: on a device with no HW H.264, the SW codec is Cisco’s downloaded binary, so before that download lands (or with the codec switched off in settings) there is nothing to open. The caller treats None as “no video this attempt”, which is the honest outcome.
run_adaptation_tick 🔒
Read the connection RTT (the one bit of IO) and advance the adaptation one tick. Splitting the RTT read out keeps apply_adaptation_tick pure + unit-testable.
send_coordinator 🔒
The process-wide coordinator the publish loops register with. A OnceLock like call_runtime — a call has one connection at a time, and the slots deregister on teardown, so no per-session construction is needed.
spawn_codec_renegotiator 🔒
Watch the roster and re-negotiate the encode codec as peers join/leave, writing the decision into adapt.target_codec for the encode loop to act on. encode_caps is probed once by the caller (the probe isn’t free) and reused here. Lives for the publish — ends when view_rx closes (session torn down).
spawn_feedback_listener 🔒
Spawn the producer side of the receiver→producer BWE loop: watch the feedback_tx broadcast and, when a report names OUR video track of source, fold its loss into AdaptShared (max-reduced across reporters this interval — serve the worst leg). The adaptation tick drains it into the sender-side rate controller. Reports for other tracks (or a stale id) are ignored. “Our track” is resolved exactly like the keyframe listener.
spawn_keyframe_listener 🔒
Spawn the producer side of the PLI relay: watch the keyframe_tx broadcast and, when the requested TrackId is OUR video track of source (Camera vs Screen), mark a PLI pending in AdaptShared. The adaptation tick drains it through the throttle, coalescing a storm into a single IDR. “Our track” is resolved from the SessionView (a video track whose peer == self AND source == source).
