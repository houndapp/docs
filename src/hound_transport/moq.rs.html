1//! # MoQ media transport over an iroh `Endpoint`
2//!
3//! The IO shell that moves encoded media over a **MoQ session** (`moq-net`)
4//! carried on a raw-QUIC **iroh** connection (`web-transport-iroh`). MoQ's
5//! group/object model owns framing and (for video) loss recovery, so the custom
6//! packetizer/reassembler in [`hound_media`] is not on the video path.
7//!
8//! ## Sans-IO boundary
9//!
10//! This shell only does IO. The sans-IO [`hound_media`] cores it sits in front of —
11//! jitter/playout ([`hound_media::PlayoutBuffer`]), the A/V clock
12//! ([`hound_media::CaptureClock`]), the BWE→encoder loop
13//! ([`hound_media::RateController`]/[`hound_media::QualityLadder`]) and AEC — are
14//! never touched here. The publisher takes encoded [`hound_media::Frame`]s (exactly
15//! what the encoder produces) and the subscriber hands back reassembled
16//! [`hound_media::Frame`]s (exactly what jitter/playout expects). The clock is
17//! injected as `now_us`.
18//!
19//! ## Frame ↔ group/object mapping
20//!
21//! ```text
22//! one media flow   →  one TRACK              (the subscribe unit)
23//! one GOP          →  one GROUP              (its own QUIC uni-stream)
24//! one encoded frame →  one OBJECT (frame)    (opaque payload, byte-for-byte)
25//! ```
26//!
27//! * **keyframe → NEW group boundary.** A keyframe finishes the open group and
28//!   `create_group`s the next sequence; the keyframe payload is the first object
29//!   in the new group.
30//! * **delta → object appended to the open keyframe group** (`write_frame`). A
31//!   delta before any keyframe is rejected — there is no decodable group to
32//!   anchor it.
33//! * **object payload is forwarded verbatim** — nothing decodes or rewrites it.
34//!
35//! ## Delivery mode & why it kills the keyframe-after-deltas freeze
36//!
37//! moq-net serves **each group on its own QUIC uni-stream**, so:
38//!
39//! * **within a GOP:** ordered + reliable (QUIC stream); a lost delta is repaired
40//!   by QUIC stream retransmit — we do **not** hand-roll NACK for video.
41//! * **across GOPs:** independent streams; an old GOP's stalled stream can never
42//!   head-of-line-block a newer one. The subscriber drains with
43//!   `TrackConsumer::next_group`, which **discards a late group at the
44//!   boundary** instead of waiting — freeze prevention expressed as transport
45//!   semantics rather than app-level NACK.
46//!
47//! Audio takes the same group/object path but rolls groups by COUNT, not
48//! keyframe: successive Opus frames ride one ordered group, rolled every few
49//! frames (see `AUDIO_GROUP_OBJECTS`).
50//!
51//! ## Join semantics: head-of-current-group replay
52//!
53//! A fresh subscriber joins at the **head of the latest group**, not at the live
54//! edge: moq-net's publisher does `start_at(track.latest())` when the SUBSCRIBE
55//! carries no `start_group` (and nothing in this stack ever sets one), and
56//! `latest()` is the *currently open* group. Since video rolls a new group on each
57//! keyframe (see [`MoqTrackPublisher::publish_frame`]), that group's object 0 is the
58//! last IDR — so the subscriber is replayed the IDR plus every delta buffered since
59//! it, then continues live. Older, *completed* groups are what it does not get. A
60//! relay in the middle preserves this: it keeps its own `TrackProducer` per track
61//! and applies the same `start_at(latest())` to each downstream subscriber.
62//!
63//! Consequences worth knowing before optimising the keyframe path:
64//! * The normal subscribe does **not** need a fresh IDR from the producer. The
65//!   receiver's keyframe gate passes on the first delivered frame.
66//! * It stops working when the open group's head is evicted — moq-net caps a group
67//!   at 1024 objects / 32 MB and drops from the front, after which the group's
68//!   stream fails `CacheFull`. Our encoders bound the GOP under that
69//!   (`max_gop_frames`), but a long static screenshare is the case to watch.
70//! * It also does not help a track with no open group at all: one that has not gone
71//!   live yet, or a simulcast layer that was just spun up. Those genuinely wait for
72//!   the producer's next IDR, which is why the client turns a `RequestKeyframe` into
73//!   a forced IDR immediately rather than on its adaptation tick.
74//!
75//! ## Live-join gate (correctness, not timing)
76//!
77//! The session handshake completing does **not** imply a SUBSCRIBE arrived, so the
78//! publisher gates first-frame production on [`MoqTrackPublisher::subscribed`]
79//! (`TrackProducer::used`) — which resolves deterministically once a consumer
80//! attaches — guaranteeing the opening keyframe (group 0) is produced with no
81//! `sleep`/yield timing dependency. Belt-and-braces given the replay above (the
82//! `max_sequence` group is never age-evicted), but it is what makes the *first*
83//! group exist at all.

85/// MoQ version-negotiation ALPN (`moq-net`'s `ALPN_LITE`). In raw mode
86/// `web-transport-iroh` reports the iroh connection's ALPN as the MoQ protocol;
87/// moq-net rejects an unrecognised ALPN with `UnknownAlpn`, so the wire ALPN is
88/// part of the MoQ handshake contract and must be a moq-net ALPN — not an
89/// invented `hound-*` one.
90pub const ALPN: &[u8] = b"moql";

92/// A default broadcast path for the peer-to-peer types. In a real call the

98/// Errors from the MoQ IO shell. Every moq-net/iroh failure is surfaced; the
99/// caller decides whether to retry the session or fail the call.
100#[derive(Debug, thiserror::Error)]
101pub enum MoqError {
102    /// The underlying iroh connection failed.
103    #[error("iroh: {0}")]
104    Iroh(String),
105    /// A moq-net session/broadcast/track/group operation failed.
106    #[error("moq: {0}")]
107    Moq(String),
108    /// A delta frame arrived before any keyframe — no decodable group to anchor
109    /// it. The publisher rejects rather than guessing a group boundary.
110    #[error("delta frame before any keyframe (no open group)")]
111    DeltaBeforeKeyframe,
112    /// A reassembled object carried a track/codec byte that is not a known
113    /// [`hound_media::TrackKind`]/[`hound_media::Codec`]. Dropped, never guessed.
114    #[error("unknown track/codec descriptor on object")]
115    UnknownDescriptor,
116    /// A reassembled object's payload exceeded the app-level per-kind bound
117    /// (`MAX_AUDIO_OBJECT`/`MAX_VIDEO_OBJECT`). Defense-in-depth on top of
118    /// moq-net's own frame cap — dropped like any other malformed object, never a
119    /// session-level error (see `decode_object`).
120    #[error("object payload exceeds the per-kind size bound")]
121    ObjectTooLarge,
122    /// The relay rejected the relay-token handshake (bad/expired/missing token,
123    /// or a namespace the token's space does not authorise).
124    #[error("relay rejected the connection (auth)")]
125    RelayAuthRejected,
126}
