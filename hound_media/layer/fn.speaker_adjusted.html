Modulate a tile’s own demand by where its peer sits in the speaking ranking — the receiver-side Last-N lever, applied as a modifier on the size-derived rung rather than as a replacement for it.

  • the dominant speaker moves up one rung (a grid cell’s THUMB becomes LOW), which is Jitsi’s onstage-preferred-height-px = 360 against its default-max-height-px = 180, expressed in this ladder’s terms;
  • a peer observed silent for the whole long window, in a crowded call, moves down one rung;
  • everyone else — including every peer we have no speaking evidence about, e.g. an un-upgraded client that never reports a level — is left exactly as the tile priced it.

Three properties make this composable with everything P1/P2 built, and each is pinned by a test below:

  1. The layer axis is invariant. Hidden and Source pass through untouched and a Capped demand stays Capped, so modulation can never turn a subscription on, off, or into a different track. It moves a cap, which the LayerHysteresis same-axis path commits immediately — so RECEIVER-side, P2’s churn bounds are not merely respected, they are not engaged (no resubscribe, no pipeline restart, no dwell consumed).

    That is only the receiver’s half, and the producer’s is not free: a sustained handover moves the cross-receiver MINIMUM cap (plan_layers), so the promoted peer’s shared low layer changes rung — an encoder rebuild plus an IDR to every subscriber of it. Bounded, not absent: at most one such rebuild per crate::GEOMETRY_REBUILD_DWELL_US per producer, which is exactly why crate::speaking::SWITCH_DWELL_US is pinned to that dwell.

  2. Never above the ladder, never below it. A promotion stops at the richest below-source rung (Source is the tile-size signal’s decision alone — a user staring at an expanded tile gets sharp video whether or not they are talking), and a demotion stops at the cheapest.

    A screen share is exempt entirely. Speaking is evidence about a face, not about shared content: whether the sharer is the one talking says nothing about how legible their slides need to be, and a share’s rungs are 900 kbps and 1.4 Mbps apart — moving one on a speaking transition would hand the producer an encoder rebuild plus an IDR to every subscriber for a signal that was never about that track. This used to be enforced by cardinality alone (a one-rung screen ladder has nothing to move); SCREEN_MID_RUNG ended that, so the carve-out is now explicit and pinned by a test below.

  3. Silence must be evidence. SpeakerTier::Unknown is never demoted; only Silent, which the ranker only reports after watching a peer for its whole long window. Same two-tier trust rule as TileDemand’s reported-vs-seeded hide.

Apply this to the TILE demand, before folding with the receiver’s own bandwidth demand (most_constrained): a promotion must never lift a receiver above what its link estimate allows.
