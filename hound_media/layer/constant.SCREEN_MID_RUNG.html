The intermediate screen rung: 720p @ 30 fps ≈ 1.1 Mbps, sitting between SCREEN_LOW_RUNG (200 kbps) and the 2.5 Mbps source.

It exists to shrink the screen ladder’s one enormous step. With 360p15 as the only below-source rung a share’s whole ladder was 200 kbps → 2.5 Mbps, and crate::allocate_video’s cheapest-first upgrade phase therefore left every budget in a 2.3 Mbps-wide band buying the share nothing at all: it went from “barely legible” straight to “more than the link has”. Two steps of 900 kbps and 1.4 Mbps instead, which is what makes a 5 Mbps receiver watching a share plus two cameras land on a sharp 720p30 share rather than a 360p15 one.

720p30 is also the legibility ladder’s own next stop — screen_rungs’ floor resolution is 720p (“below that text is unreadable”) — so the intermediate rung is legible content at a real frame rate, not a compromise between the two.

It is a BUDGET rung, not a SIZE rung. A tile’s own size never asks for it: tile_quality_request stops a screen tile’s size-derived walk at SCREEN_LOW_RUNG’s coverage, so a share’s size demand is still exactly floor-or-Source. This rung is reachable only through crate::allocate_video — as an upgrade step a Source-demanding share takes when the budget affords 1.1 Mbps but not 2.5, and as the step a deficit walks back down through. The reason is the coalesced minimum, and it is load-bearing: a Source demand is immune to it (plan_layers routes it to the source track), a Capped demand is not, so a stage share that asked for Capped(1.1 Mbps) alongside anyone rendering a grid-cell share would be folded down to the 360p15 floor and rendered 2× upscaled — which is the legibility failure SCREEN_LOW_RUNG exists to prevent, inflicted on the one receiver actually reading the text. See tile_quality_request’s walk.
