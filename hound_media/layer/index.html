Module layer 

Source
Expand description

The video layer model — the single source of truth mapping a layer combo to a moq track name + wire TrackKind, and the pure helpers that turn a receiver’s tile size / bandwidth into a QualityRequest.

Two scalability axes, deliberately mapped to two different transport mechanisms:

  • Spatial (SpatialLayer) = a SEPARATE moq track. The relay is forward-no- transcode + group-atomic, so per-receiver selection is per-track subscription; the source layer keeps its original track name (an un-upgraded peer never learns about layers and keeps working), and the low layer is an additional track a constrained / small-tile receiver subscribes to instead.
  • Temporal (TemporalLayer) = a per-FRAME marking WITHIN one spatial track (a base frame is self-decodable; an enhancement frame depends on base and is droppable). Not part of the track identity — see the producer-side gating.

Keeping this here (not in call.rs) keeps the wire/string juggling in one tested place and out of the IO glue.

Constants§

CAM_SOURCE_BPS
What the source rung costs — the top-rung bitrate the client publishes this content at (CAM_BITRATE / SCREEN_BITRATE in crates/app/client/src/call/mod.rs, and the source_bps the producer’s crate::plan_layers coalesces against).
LOW_RUNG
The low rung: a mid-sized tile that is still below source. 640×360 @ 20 fps ≈ 420 kbps — the old cam_l0 geometry, re-priced (it was 1 Mbps at 30 fps).
SCREEN_LOW_RUNG
The only below-source screen rung. Screen share degrades by DegradePriority::Legibility — drop frames before pixels — so a small share tile keeps 360p (a 180p thumbnail of shared text is unreadable, where a 180p thumbnail of a face is fine) and gives up frame rate instead: 15 fps is the same floor the screen ladder’s legibility ordering uses. ≈200 kbps against the 1 Mbps it used to cost, at identical resolution.
SCREEN_SOURCE_BPS
SPEAKER_DEMOTE_MIN_PEERS
How many remote video peers a call must have before a peer that has been observed silent is priced a rung below what its tile alone would buy.
THUMB_RUNG
The thumbnail rung: a grid cell, a filmstrip stamp, an off-screen tile. 320×180 @ 15 fps ≈ 130 kbps — LiveKit ships h180 at this geometry, Jitsi’s bridge defaults its thumbnails to 180p, and Daily publishes 320×180 @ 10 fps. Against the 1 Mbps flat low rung this replaces it is 4× fewer pixels and half the frames, which is where the large-call receive-CPU win comes from.
TILE_SOURCE_MIN_PIXELS
A visible tile larger than this — in physical pixels — takes the source rather than any below-source rung: it is past what the richest low rung (LOW_RUNG, and SCREEN_LOW_RUNG at the same geometry) can fill within TILE_UPSCALE_TOLERANCE_NUM/TILE_UPSCALE_TOLERANCE_DEN.
TILE_UPSCALE_TOLERANCE_DEN
TILE_UPSCALE_TOLERANCE_NUM
How far a rung may be stretched to fill a tile before the next rung up is worth its extra bits — an area ratio of 3/2, i.e. ~22% of linear upscale in each dimension. Expressed as an integer fraction so the boundary is exact.

low_rung_for_cap
Invert a coalesced cap back into the rung the producer should encode: the richest rung this cap can pay for, never below the cheapest one.
low_rungs 🔒
The below-source rungs available for source, cheapest first. Every caller walks this in order; the ascending-price invariant is asserted in debug builds by debug_assert_ascending.
most_constrained
Fold two demands into the more-constrained one. Hidden beats everything (a tile nobody is looking at cannot become one that is by way of a bandwidth estimate), Capped beats Source, and two of a kind take the lower cap. Used to combine a tile-size demand with a bandwidth demand so the receiver can never over-request beyond what either signal allows.
rung_bps
What one below-source rung costs for this content, from the bits-per-pixel model in crate::congestion. Camera and screen are priced by different models: screen content (text, flat regions, sharp edges) compresses several times better than motion video, so pricing a shared window like a talking head would oversize it.
source_bps
The price of taking the SOURCE track for this content — see CAM_SOURCE_BPS.
speaker_adjusted
Modulate a tile’s own demand by where its peer sits in the speaking ranking — the receiver-side Last-N lever, applied as a modifier on the size-derived rung rather than as a replacement for it.
tile_quality_request
Map a rendered tile’s physical pixel size + visibility to a QualityRequest. This is the demand half of demand-driven simulcast — it never asks for more than the tile can use, which is exactly the receiver-CPU win (a thumbnail pulls the low track).
track_kind
The wire TrackKind for a (source, spatial) pair.
track_name
The moq track_name for a (source, spatial) pair — the subscribe/publish key under the per-peer broadcast path. The source layers keep the original names so a peer that never learns about layers is unaffected.
