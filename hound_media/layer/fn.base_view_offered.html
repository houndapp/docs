pub fn base_view_offered(source: VideoSource, spatial: SpatialLayer) -> bool
Expand description

Whether a (source, spatial) track has a base-layer-only sibling the receiver may subscribe to — i.e. whether the SFU derives a …_t0 for it.

Camera source track only, and both halves of that are deliberate:

  • Camera, not screen. Screen degrades by DegradePriority::Legibility — frames before pixels — and its ladder has already spent that budget: SCREEN_LOW_RUNG is 15 fps and SCREEN_MID_RUNG is the sharp rung a reader wants. Halving a 15 fps share to 7.5 fps is a slideshow of someone’s scrolling editor, and halving the 30 fps mid rung lands on the same 15 fps the floor already offers at a fifth of the price. There is no step here worth a second derived track.
  • The source track, not _l0. The gap the camera ladder has no rung for is LOW_RUNG (420 kbps) → source (1.5 Mbps) — the same “one enormous step” shape SCREEN_MID_RUNG was built for. Below that, _l0’s rungs are already at the frame-rate floor for a recognisable face (the thumbnail is 15 fps; halved it is 7.5), and every extra derived track puts the SFU on the per-object path for another stream — the cost this design is otherwise careful to keep proportional to what is actually watched.

So one derived track per camera publisher, buying the ladder a 720p rung at half frame rate for ~70% of the source’s price.
