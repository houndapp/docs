pub fn downlink_deficient(tracks: &[TrackDelivery]) -> bool
Expand description

DEFICIENT, in LiveKit’s StreamAllocator sense: any subscribed track is failing to deliver what this receiver ASKED IT FOR. One starved track is enough — the budget is shared, so an overshoot shows up wherever the link happens to drop it first, not democratically across every track.

§Why the comparison is against the allocation and not against the source

“This track has capped below its source rung” is the wrong predicate, and it fails in the direction that blanks a call. A track’s cap is a per-track, per-sender-link fact: one remote peer on hotel wifi is capped for as long as they stay on it, and one uplink-constrained sender’s frames never arrive for ANY receiver. Read as evidence about this receiver’s downlink it is both sticky and undamped — the predicate stays true forever, the budget sheds 15% every sustain window, and a 32-tile gallery walks to the 300 kbps floor in about sixteen seconds with thirty of its tiles hidden, because of one peer nobody was watching closely.

The receiver-side question is narrower and answerable: is this track delivering what the budget bought it? A peer allocated the thumbnail rung and delivering the thumbnail rung is not evidence of anything, however far below its own source that is. Only a track delivering strictly LESS than its allocation says the receiver asked for more than it can take — which is exactly what the budget is a controller for.
