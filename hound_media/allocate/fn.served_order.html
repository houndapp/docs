pub fn served_order(
    tiles: &[TileRequest],
    decode_budget: Option<usize>,
) -> Vec<usize>
Expand description

The tiles this receiver actually serves, as indices into tiles, in the priority order allocate_video serves them in. Tiles whose own demand is already Hidden are excluded (they subscribe to nothing); decode_budget truncates the rest to its k highest-priority entries.

This is THE definition of the served set, and it is public because the decode cut is not the only thing that has to agree with it. The reconcile loop prices its BudgetObservation — the demand ceiling the AIMD probes toward and the floor cost it seeds and dwells against — and both figures have to describe the tiles that will actually subscribe. Priced over the pre-cut set instead, a 32-tile gallery cut to 16 seeds its bandwidth budget with 32 floors and probes toward 32 demands: the controller would be sized for a receive leg that does not exist, and the join seed alone would hand out twice the budget the survivors can spend. One function, both callers, so the cut and the pricing cannot disagree.
