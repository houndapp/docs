§Receiver-side total-budget video allocator

Every other demand signal in this crate is per track: a tile prices its own rung (crate::layer::tile_quality_request), a subscription prices its own delivery (crate::downlink::DownlinkDemand), and the producer coalesces whatever arrives (crate::plan_layers). Nothing anywhere looks at the SUM. N tiles can each individually fit a link while their total overshoots it by a multiple — which is the shape of every large-call collapse: 31 tiles that each “only” want the source rung want 46 Mbps between them.

This module is the missing cross-tile decision. allocate_video takes every visible remote tile’s demand plus one total VideoBudget and hands each tile back a QualityRequest that is never richer than what it asked for and whose sum fits the budget. It is the receive-side twin of crate::send_budget::allocate_send_budget, and it is what Zoom’s ~2 Mbps 25-tile ceiling and LiveKit’s StreamAllocator (rank tracks, distribute the estimated downlink, expose STABLE/DEFICIENT) both are.

§What it does NOT replace

  • the per-track delivery cap. crate::downlink::DownlinkDemand still folds in afterwards via crate::most_constrained — the allocator budgets what this receiver asks for, the downlink decider observes what it actually gets, and the more constrained of the two wins. A budget cannot know a single track is wedged, and a per-track observation cannot know 31 healthy tracks are 31 too many.
  • the producer’s cross-receiver minimum. plan_layers still coalesces every receiver’s cap into ONE shared low layer, so a receiver asking for a richer rung than its neighbours still does not get one. See the trade notes on plan_layers.

§Rung space, not bitrate space

The output is a rung (or the source), never an interpolated bitrate. That is what keeps the allocator compatible with crate::layer::low_rung_for_cap on the producer side, and it is why re-running it every reconcile tick does not chatter: a budget that drifts by a few kbps lands on the same rung, so the downstream LayerHysteresis sees no change at all. A raw-bps allocator would re-request on every tick.

Sans-IO: time enters only as an explicit now_us, and the allocation itself is a pure function of its inputs.

cpu_crisis_cap
The ceiling a receiver that is short of CPU may spend a freed budget up to, as a cap a caller can fold into a demand with most_constrained.
demand_ceiling_bps
The total price of the SERVED tiles’ own demands — the ceiling VideoBudget probes toward. It gates the PROBE and never truncates the estimate: climbing past what anyone actually wants would climb open-loop against no signal at all (exactly what RateController::on_feedback_idle exists to prevent on the send side), but a momentarily low ceiling — a grid scrolled entirely off screen, a peer who turned their camera off — says nothing about the link.
downlink_deficient
DEFICIENT, in LiveKit’s StreamAllocator sense: any subscribed track is failing to deliver what this receiver ASKED IT FOR. One starved track is enough — the budget is shared, so an overshoot shows up wherever the link happens to drop it first, not democratically across every track.
floor 🔒
The cheapest rung this content has — the floor every visible tile is guaranteed, clamped by the tile’s own demand so the allocator can never hand out MORE than was asked for (a bandwidth-crushed Capped(1) stays at 1).
floor_cost_bps
What it costs to give every SERVED tile its cheapest rung — the output of allocate_video’s floor phase, priced. The budget controller needs it for two unrelated jobs: it is the target the join-time seed reaches for (BudgetObservation::floor_bps), and it is the line the demote dwell measures “below” against (BudgetConfig::demote_dwell_us).
hidden 🔒
The Hidden a demoted tile reports: not rendered, carrying the cap it would have asked for. That carried value is load-bearing — plan_layers uses it to size the shared low layer when every requester is hidden, which is what keeps the rung published so an un-demotion is a subscribe rather than a producer spin-up.
next_step 🔒
The next richer option above at, never above demand. None = at is already everything this tile asked for.
price 🔒
What one ALLOCATION costs against the budget — request_bps plus the temporal discount. This module reads better at every call site with the short name.
priority 🔒
The total order the allocator serves tiles in. Total and deterministic: every component is derived from the input, and the last one is unique, so the same input always produces the same output (no map iteration order reaches a decision).
request_bps
What one allocated request costs against the budget. Hidden is free — it subscribes to nothing (call::subscribe::pick_layer).
served_order
The tiles this receiver actually serves, as indices into tiles, in the priority order allocate_video serves them in. Tiles whose own demand is already Hidden are excluded (they subscribe to nothing); decode_budget truncates the rest to its k highest-priority entries.
