Expand description

8 Mbps, ≈60 thumbnail rungs — the ceiling on the whole controller, and it wears two hats deliberately rather than being split into two numbers that would have to be kept in a fixed relation to each other:

  • the join seed (BudgetObservation::floor_bps) may raise a fresh budget to whatever its tiles’ floors cost, up to here. Without that, a 40-peer join spends its first pass at VIDEO_BUDGET_START_BPS — under the 5.2 Mbps those forty thumbnails cost — so the deficit phase hides ten tiles, and because a fresh LayerHysteresis has no history to debounce against, all ten blank immediately and ramp back one tick at a time. Seeding to the floor makes the receiver’s first answer “everyone small”, which is what the floor phase exists to say.
  • the hard cap on the probe. The budget is spent in PRICED rungs, and priced spend systematically overstates received bits (a Capped tile receives the producer’s cross-receiver-minimum shared layer, which is usually cheaper than the cap it asked for), so a clean receiver can climb past what its link has ever evidenced. BudgetObservation::ceiling_bps does not stop that: it is a DEMAND ceiling, and 60 tiles asking for the source raise it to 90 Mbps. 8 Mbps is above any gallery a real receiver renders and below any link this could mislead.
