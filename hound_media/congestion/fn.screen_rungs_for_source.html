screen_rungs re-priced for the share’s real capture geometry.

The rung table is built from nominal 16:9 candidate heights because the bitrate model needs a reference geometry — but the encoder never upscales past the source and always follows the source’s own aspect (crate::adapt’s fit_encode_dims). Left unfitted, a 900×500 window share still offers a “1080p60 = 4 Mbps” top rung: the AIMD climbs to it and hands 4 Mbps to an encoder pinned at 900×500 — roughly 8× the bits that geometry needs, spent on the QUIC connection audio shares (and paid again as receive-side packet CPU).

So each rung is re-priced by the pixels it will ACTUALLY encode (fit_encode_dims(source, rung.height)) and its reported geometry becomes that encode geometry. Rungs that collapse onto the same fitted geometry+fps produce the same rate and are deduplicated, so a source smaller than the ladder’s floor height simply yields a shorter ladder — the correct outcome, since those rungs were never distinguishable at that capture size.
