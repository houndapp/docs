§The receive-side total-video-budget controller

AIMD on the receiver’s total video budget, driven by a DEFICIENT/STABLE verdict (downlink_deficient) over what each subscribed track delivered versus what crate::allocate_video granted it. VideoBudget climbs additively (one thumbnail rung per probe), backs off multiplicatively on a sustained deficit, seeds a fresh join to the tiles’ floor cost, and holds a demote dwell so a sawtoothing estimate never blinks the bottom tiles.

Constants§

MIN_VIDEO_BUDGET_BPS
The budget floor: two thumbnail rungs, rounded up. Below this a receiver is not having a video call, and letting the AIMD walk further down would only feed the deficit phase tiles to blank.
VIDEO_BUDGET_START_BPS
Starting total video budget: 4 Mbps, which is the figure LiveKit publishes for a 25-person meeting and roughly double Zoom’s quoted 2.0 Mbps for a 25-tile gallery. It is a probe, not a measurement — VideoBudget walks it up and down from here — but the starting point decides what a 32-peer join costs in its first second, which is the moment a call is most fragile.
VIDEO_BUDGET_START_CAP_BPS
8 Mbps, ≈60 thumbnail rungs — the ceiling on the whole controller, and it wears two hats deliberately rather than being split into two numbers that would have to be kept in a fixed relation to each other:

Functions§

downlink_deficient
DEFICIENT, in LiveKit’s StreamAllocator sense: any subscribed track is failing to deliver what this receiver ASKED IT FOR. One starved track is enough — the budget is shared, so an overshoot shows up wherever the link happens to drop it first, not democratically across every track.
