§Inter-arrival jitter estimation → playout depth

JitterEstimator tracks the link’s actual jitter (the RFC 3550 smoothed mean plus a windowed tail peak) and recommends a PlayoutBuffer depth; AdaptiveJitter ramps a buffer toward that recommendation in bounded, asymmetric steps (grow fast, shrink slow). Sans-IO: arrivals enter as explicit (now_us, frame_ts_us) pairs, so the policy is deterministic.

Structs§

AdaptiveJitter
Drives a PlayoutBuffer’s depth from measured jitter: folds each frame’s arrival into a JitterEstimator and ramps the buffer toward the recommended depth in bounded, asymmetric steps. The IO shell just calls Self::on_frame per received frame; all the policy lives here, sans-IO.
JitterEstimator
Sans-IO inter-arrival jitter estimator that recommends a PlayoutBuffer depth.

Constants§

MAX_ADAPTIVE_DEPTH_US 🔒
Absolute cap (µs) on the depth AdaptiveJitter::with_floor will grow to. Deliberately NOT a multiple of the floor: tying it to the floor made the headroom above the anchor shrink with the latency target, so the tighter the floor the less tail a link was allowed to have. 450 ms is the point past which a call has stopped being conversational — beyond it, added depth buys smoothness nobody wants — and it comfortably covers the recurring ~100 ms stalls the peak tracker provisions for.
PEAK_WINDOW_US 🔒
How long an observed transit-deviation PEAK keeps the recommended depth provisioned, on the arrival clock. The smoothed mean J forgets a rare spike in under a second, but the spikes that actually pop audio are RECURRING tail events — a ~100 ms connection stall every 30–60 s (a video IDR/delta burst bufferbloating the uplink delays every stream at once; stream priority cannot reorder a downstream router’s queue). If the buffer relaxed between events, every recurrence would pop again; holding the peak for 90 s covers the observed recurrence interval, while a link that stays clean for 90 s relaxes back to floor + 4·J.
