The WebTransport leg: the preferred transport, and off unless configured.

Enabled by either a certificate pair (RT_WT_CERT + RT_WT_KEY) or the dev self-signed flag (RT_WT_SELF_SIGNED). With neither, no UDP socket is bound and /realtime/config advertises nothing — clients go straight to the WebSocket, which is a fully supported configuration, not a degraded one.

§Admission is per peer

The accept loop’s only job is to hand a peer to a task of its own. Everything that waits on the peer — the QUIC/HTTP/3 handshake, reading its credential, writing the accept or the refusal, and its opening of the control stream — happens inside that task under [hound_wt::DEFAULT_ADMISSION_TIMEOUT]. A peer that stalls therefore costs one task for ten seconds and nothing else; doing the handshake inline would let it park every connection queued behind it.

The deadline bounds admission and nothing else. A session that reaches hub::run_session has already proved itself and runs unbounded, exactly as the WebSocket leg’s does — so admit hands its parts back rather than running the session inside the timeout, which would cap every session at the admission deadline.

§Supervision

The accept loop and the certificate watcher are long-lived tasks whose death is silent: no accept loop means no new WebTransport session ever again, on a process that otherwise looks healthy. Both are therefore run under supervise, which restarts them with capped backoff and counts every restart.

Constants§

CERT_POLL_INTERVAL 🔒
How often the certificate files are checked for rotation. Slow, because it is a poll against a change that happens monthly at most; a rotation taking up to this long to apply costs nothing (existing connections keep their session, and the old certificate is still valid until it expires).
DEV_SANS 🔒
Subject alternative names on the dev self-signed certificate. Loopback only: a self-signed endpoint is reachable only by a client that pinned its hash out of /realtime/config, which is a development affordance.
MAX_RESTART_BACKOFF 🔒
Ceiling on the restart backoff. A wedged dependency must not be hammered, but the realtime plane must not stay down for minutes either.
MIN_RESTART_BACKOFF 🔒
First backoff after a supervised task dies, doubling to MAX_RESTART_BACKOFF.
